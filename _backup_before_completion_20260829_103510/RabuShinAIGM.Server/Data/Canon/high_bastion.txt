CHAPTER 5
                    HIGH BASTION
             SIEGE OF STONE AND STEEL
   Recommended Levels 6-7 • Starting Level 6 • Hybrid Milestone Advancement
Chapter Purpose. High Bastion shifts the campaign from investigation to open
warfare. The characters discover that Rabu Shin is testing coordinated Krasis
infantry against a disciplined fortress. They must expose tunnel infiltrators, defend
the walls, preserve the inner gate, and defeat a regenerating commander designed
to learn from military resistance. Characters should reach 7th level after breaking
the siege and recovering proof that Silverreach interests funded the assault.


                             High Bastion settlement map


High Bastion
High Bastion is a mountain fortress built into a sheer ridge overlooking the
continent’s central trade roads. Its concentric walls, high towers, cliffside barracks,
and iron-bound gates have withstood armies, monsters, and winter storms. The
garrison prides itself on discipline. Yet the enemy now probing its defenses does
not raid for food or territory. It studies patrol routes, withdraws before traps close,
and changes tactics after every skirmish.
                                ARRIVAL AT HIGH BASTION
   High Bastion rises from the mountain like a crown of black stone. Banners
   whip above battlements crowded with soldiers. Far below, switchback roads
   cling to the cliffs. A horn sounds once, then twice. Archers rush to the western
   wall as a boulder strikes the outer gate with a crack like thunder. It does not
   roll from the slope. Something below has thrown it.


Starting the Chapter
 • The Conclave’s Warning. Archmage Vaelis sends a sealed letter explaining that
    lattice formations have appeared in military patterns around High Bastion.
 • A Desperate Courier. A wounded scout reaches Lunareth with a request from
   Captain Valric Stonehand: “Bring anyone who has fought these things before.”
 • The Silverreach Connection. Records recovered beneath Lunareth mention
    payments routed through House Varin to mercenary companies operating near
    the fortress.
 • The Northern Road. The party’s route toward Frostharbor naturally passes
    through High Bastion, where all civilian traffic has been halted.
 Chapter Flow: The party enters during a siege alert, meets the war council, and
 investigates attacks occurring both outside and beneath the fortress. The middle of the
 chapter presents several defensive missions that influence the final assault. The finale is
 a multi-stage battle at the inner gate against Krasis Ogres, disciplined hybrid soldiers, and
 the Krasis Commander.


Keyed Locations in High Bastion
1. The Outer Gate. A layered gatehouse overlooking the mountain road. Its
murder holes, winches, and ballista platforms are the first target of every assault.
2. Bastion Bazaar. A crowded courtyard now serving as a refugee camp,
infirmary, and supply depot.
3. Ironpeak Armory. Weapons and armor are repaired here under Quartermaster
Brunna Flint. Several shipments bear false Silverreach seals.
4. WyvernGuard Tavern. An officers’ tavern where scouts trade reports and
frightened soldiers speak more honestly than they do at council.


5. Council Hall. Captain Valric Stonehand coordinates the defense from a chamber
covered in geometric attack maps.
6. Cliffwatch Tower. A damaged watchtower above a hidden ascent route. Its
signal crystal has been sabotaged.
7. The Inner Gate. The fortress’s final defensive line and the site of the chapter’s
climactic battle.
8. Underkeep Tunnels. Old drainage and mining passages beneath the fortress.
Krasis forces are using them to bypass the walls.

                                  THE PATTERN OF WAR
     The council table is buried beneath maps, casualty lists, and broken pieces of
     strange bone armor. Red markers surround the fortress in precise arcs.
     Captain Stonehand places one final marker at the inner gate. “They are not
     trying to take the Bastion,” he says. “They are measuring how we stop them.”

completed mission grants one Defensive Advantage during the final battle. The

Part 2: The Tunnel Breach
                                  SOMETHING BENEATH
     The passage descends through raw mountain stone. Fresh claw marks score
     the floor, but the walls are smoother than mining tools could make them. A
     low vibration travels through your boots. Dust spills from the ceiling in a
     straight line, moving toward you.


Two Ankhegs burst from the tunnel floor. One has plates of grafted iron and bone,
increasing its Armor Class to 16. They are not natural allies; a lattice beacon
embedded in the wall compels them to attack in coordinated turns.


 • Collapsing Supports. A creature adjacent to a timber support can use an action
    and make a DC 13 Athletics check to collapse a 10-foot section after it moves
    clear.
 • Acid Channels. Ankheg acid opens narrow runoff channels. A creature entering
   one takes 3 (1d6) acid damage.
 • Beacon Crystal. AC 13, 15 hit points. Destroying it removes the ankhegs’
   advantage from Pack Coordination.
 Tunnel Evidence: Beyond the breach, the party finds supply crates marked with House
 Varin’s silver hawk. Their contents include concentrated nutrient paste, restraint chains,
 and notes measuring “fortification response time.”


Part 3: The Regenerating Prisoner
A captured hybrid lies chained in the infirmary. It resembles a human soldier
beneath segmented chitin and grafted muscle. Its wounds close slowly while the
chaplain watches. The prisoner does not plead. It studies everyone who enters.
                                 THE PRISONER LEARNS
   The prisoner’s eyes move from sword to spell focus to holy symbol. “Radiant
   response observed,” it says. “Flame response observed. Command pattern
   updated.” The chains tighten as new bone ridges grow beneath its wrists.
   “Thank you.”


Run this as a tense interrogation. A DC 15 Insight check reveals that every question
teaches the creature something about the fortress. A DC 14 Arcana or Medicine
check identifies a crystal node at the base of its skull. Removing or destroying the
node stops its regeneration and frees the prisoner’s original mind for a few final
moments.
 • The prisoner was once Lieutenant Corren Vale, captured six weeks earlier.
 • He remembers a “surgeon with glass hands” directing the transformation.
 • The assault commander is called the Stone-Breaker and regenerates unless
    struck by fire or radiant energy.
 • A second force waits beyond the ridge to record the outcome, not to join the
   siege.


Part 4: Defensive Missions
The Broken Ballista
The replacement winch lies in a wrecked supply cart outside the western wall.
Four hobgoblins and one hobgoblin captain guard it. They are mercenaries, not


Krasis. Captured mercenaries reveal they were paid through a Silverreach broker
and ordered to keep the fortress occupied, not conquer it.

Cliffwatch Signal
The tower is occupied by two gargoyles altered with climbing hooks and a lattice
saboteur using spy statistics. Restoring the beacon requires three successes before
two failures using Arcana DC 14, Investigation DC 13, or smith’s tools DC 12. Failure
triggers a lightning discharge dealing 3d6 lightning damage to creatures in the
tower.

Mountain Wyvern
A wyvern circles supply caravans below the fortress. It is not corrupted, but a
crystal barb lodged beneath its wing drives it into a frenzy. Removing the barb
after reducing the wyvern below half its hit points ends the encounter peacefully
and earns the garrison’s gratitude. Killing it provides enough venom for two doses
of wyvern poison.

Avalanche Run
A patrol is trapped beneath unstable snow and stone. Run a four-success skill
challenge using Athletics, Survival, Nature, or appropriate magic. Each failure
inflicts 2d6 bludgeoning damage on a rescuer or costs one trapped soldier. Saving
at least four soldiers adds twenty guards to the final defense.

Spy in Steel
One officer at the war council is a disguised lattice agent. Clues include mirrored
handwriting, no reaction to cold, and crystalline blood beneath a bandaged palm.
Exposing the spy prevents the commander from knowing the party’s exact
capabilities in advance.


Part 5: Siege of the Inner Gate
                                     THE ASSAULT BEGINS
     Night falls without sunset. Smoke from burning pitch turns the sky black while
     horns answer one another across the mountain. Then comes the impact: one
     strike, then another, each perfectly timed. The outer gate buckles. Soldiers
     shout from the walls. Through the smoke emerge towering shapes carrying
     shields grown from bone and iron. Behind them marches a figure whose torn
     flesh closes with every step.


Running the Siege
The siege occurs in three waves. The party has one round between waves to move,
drink potions, or issue orders. At initiative count 20, roll on the Siege Event table.

Wave One: The Shield Line
Four Krasis Soldiers advance behind two shield-grafted Krasis Ogres. The soldiers
use the ogres as mobile cover and focus attacks on anyone attempting to reach the
gate winches. The ogres attempt to deal structural damage rather than pursue
fleeing characters.


Wave Two: The Breach Team
Two additional Krasis Soldiers enter with three cult engineers carrying lattice
charges. A charge placed against the gate explodes at the end of the following
round, dealing 2 structural damage unless removed with an action and a DC 14
Arcana or thieves’ tools check.

Wave Three: The Stone-Breaker
                              THE KRASIS COMMANDER
   The battlefield quiets for a heartbeat. A broad figure steps through the smoke
   wearing armor fused directly into its skin. One eye is human. The other glows
   with a rotating green sigil. It looks at the broken defenders, then at you.
   “Resistance profile confirmed,” it says. “Commencing refinement.”


The Krasis Commander enters with any surviving enemies. It prioritizes the
character who dealt the most damage in the previous wave and changes targets
when a creature demonstrates fire or radiant attacks. If the party completed the
prisoner scene, they know how to suppress its regeneration.

Victory Conditions
 • The Krasis Commander is defeated.
 • The inner gate retains at least 1 structural point, or the party prevents enemies
    from crossing the courtyard for three rounds after it falls.
 • At least one senior defender survives: Stonehand, Steelhand, or Chaplain
   Mirelda.
 • The party recovers the commander’s tactical journal or intact cranial lattice.


Aftermath
                                   AFTER THE SIEGE
   Dawn reveals a battlefield arranged with terrible precision. The dead lie in
   lines. Broken bone shields point toward the gate like compass needles. Captain
   Stonehand kneels beside the commander’s remains and lifts a crystal slate
   from its chest. “This was never an invasion,” he says. “We were an
   examination.”


The tactical journal records response times, casualty ratios, damage types, and the
party’s combat habits. One section bears the seal of House Varin in Silverreach.
Another entry reads: “Fortification breach efficiency improving. Urban compliance
at 42 percent. Awaiting amphibious relay confirmation.” This points toward both
Silverreach and Marrowfen.


Rewards
 • Stonehand pays the party 750 gp from the fortress war chest.
 • Each character receives the Bastion Defender’s Mark, granting advantage on one
   Charisma check with Vael Turog soldiers or officials.
 • The party receives one suit of +1 armor or one +1 shield chosen from the
    armory, subject to DM approval.
 • If the wyvern was spared, it later appears during a mountain journey and drives
    off one random encounter.
 • The commander’s lattice core is a campaign key and provides advantage on one
    Arcana check made to disable a Krasis relay.

Milestone Advancement
Characters advance to 7th level after they defend High Bastion, defeat the Krasis
Commander, and secure evidence of the Silverreach funding network. If the gate
fell but the fortress survived, advancement still occurs; the damaged Bastion
becomes a visible consequence in later chapters.

If High Bastion Falls
 • Trade between the western settlements and Silverreach is severed.
 • House Varin presents the defeat as proof that traditional armies are obsolete.
 • Refugees crowd Greymoor Hollow and Lunareth.
 • Krasis forces gain a military staging ground, adding one elite enemy to a later
   Silverreach or Marrowfen encounter.

Transition to Marrowfen and Silverreach
                               TWO ROADS FORWARD
   The crystal slate projects two routes across a relief map. One line descends
   southeast into black marshland, pulsing in time with a distant relay. The other
   runs east into Silverreach and ends beneath the crest of House Varin.
   Stonehand folds his arms. “One route finds the weapons,” he says. “The other
   finds who paid for them.”

 has at least 1 hit point. This trait does not function on the turn after it takes fire

 Multiattack. The commander makes three attacks: two with its lattice blade

Mutation Surge (Recharge 5-6). The commander chooses one effect: gain 20

REACTIONS

would hit it.

Advance. The commander moves up to half its speed.
Blade Attack. The commander makes one lattice blade attack.
Tactical Reversal (Costs 2 Actions). One creature the commander can see

feet in a direction chosen by the commander.
