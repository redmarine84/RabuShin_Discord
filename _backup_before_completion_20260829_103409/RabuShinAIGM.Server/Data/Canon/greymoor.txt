Chapter 1
          GREYMOOR HOLLOW
              THE SHEEP THAT HOWLED
   Recommended Levels 1–3 • Starting Level 1 • Hybrid Milestone Advancement
Chapter Purpose. Greymoor Hollow introduces the Krasis, establishes the
repeated lattice motifs, and turns ordinary rural fears into evidence of deliberate
transformation. Characters should reach 3rd level after resolving the Missing
Shepherd and confronting the Shepherd Krasis.


                           Greymoor Hollow settlement map


Greymoor Hollow
Greymoor Hollow is a practical farming settlement of low stone walls, timber
cottages, grazing pastures, and crop fields divided by a narrow stream. Its people
are cautious with outsiders but quick to cooperate when their harvest is
threatened. Recent events have strained that reserve: livestock breathe incorrectly,
corn rots before harvest, tools corrode near the stream, and a faint green shimmer
sometimes moves along the waterline after dark.

Starting the Chapter
                          WHY THE ADVENTURERS ARE HERE
   The Lowroad bends between stone fences and fields yellowed by late summer.
   Greymoor Hollow lies less than a mile ahead, its chimney smoke rising in thin
   gray columns. You have come seeking work, answers, or simply a safe roof
   before nightfall. Then the birds fall silent. In the tall grass beside the road,
   something begins to growl.


Part 1: The Lowroad Ambush
                               THE WOLF REVEALS ITSELF
   A broken cart wheel lies half-buried in the roadside dust. Beyond it, the grass
   shivers without wind. A low, strained growl rises from the ditch—wet, uneven,
   and followed by a second breath from the same throat. A shape lunges into the
   road. It is a wolf, but one shoulder bulges beneath stretched hide, and a half-
   formed foreleg twitches against its ribs. Its eyes glow faintly green.


Encounter: Partially Mutated Wolf. Use the wolf stat block. The mutation is
cosmetic and does not alter its challenge rating. The wolf begins 20 feet from the
nearest character and attempts to knock isolated targets prone with its bite and
Pack Tactics only if another hostile creature becomes involved.
Terrain. The road is 15 feet wide. Tall grass on each side is lightly obscured and
counts as difficult terrain for Small creatures. The broken wheel provides half
cover.

Developments
 • At half hit points, the wolf releases a warped howl that echoes too long. A distant
   answer comes from the direction of Greymoor’s pastures.
 • A character examining the body with a successful DC 12 Arcana check detects
   residual transmutation magic beneath the skin.
 • A successful DC 12 Investigation check reveals a faint geometric scar burned
   beneath the wolf’s fur.
 • The abandoned cart contains grain sacks, a broken lantern, and a bloodstained
    farmer’s cloak. Tracks indicate the driver fled toward town.


                                  AFTER THE AMBUSH
   The creature collapses in the dust. Its malformed limb twitches once and
   becomes still. Beneath the patchy fur, a precise geometric scar has been burned
   into the flesh. The air around the body smells faintly of wet iron. Beyond the
   road, the fields remain unnaturally quiet—and Greymoor Hollow waits less
   than a mile ahead.


Continuing to Greymoor Hollow
                           ARRIVAL AT GREYMOOR HOLLOW
   The final stretch of road passes silent sheep pastures and cornfields whose
   outer rows have turned gray. The village sign creaks above the road:
   GREYMOOR HOLLOW — HARVEST ENDURES. Farmers stop their work as
   you pass. A mother pulls her child indoors. Somewhere behind a barn, a sheep
   bleats with a sound that almost resembles a human cough.


The villagers direct armed strangers to Mayor Harlowe, who has taken a table at
the Mudhaven Inn to hear reports. Characters may visit other locations first, but
the mayor provides the clearest route into the chapter.


Keyed Locations in Greymoor Hollow
1. Mudhaven Inn — A warm but tense public house run by Hilda Mudhaven, a
practical human commoner with 12 hit points. Farmers exchange rumors here,
and Mayor Harlowe uses the back room for emergency meetings.
2. Shepherd’s Croft — Young Tomas’s home and the epicenter of the livestock
mutations. Sheep pens, a low barn, and a ruined pasture surround the croft.
3. Raff’s Smithy — Raff, a lawful good dwarf using the guard stat block, distrusts
magic. Tools left near the stream corrode in patterned lines.
4. Farmer’s Market — Several stalls remain closed. Grain appears sound from the
outside but is hollow within. This location begins The Well That Whispers and The
Crimson Cornfield.
5. Brimblecup’s Sundries — A traveling natural philosopher recently purchased
preserving salts, lamp oil, wire, and glass vials. The buyer was a Krasis scout.
6. Old Horn Apothecary — The herbalist has seen doubled fish gills and a green
shimmer in water samples. She offers two healing potions at half price in exchange
for help.
7. Fletcher’s Cottage — Humanoid tracks behind the cottage pace in repeating
geometric patterns before turning toward the stream.
8. Eldric’s Farm — Dying crop circles, black milk, moving farm tools, and the
silent scarecrow make this farm a recurring center of the chapter.

Part 2: Mayor Harlowe’s Concern
                                  THE MAYOR SPEAKS
   Mayor Harlowe removes his hat as you approach. Deep shadows sit beneath
   his eyes, and a stack of written complaints lies untouched beside his ale. “You
   came down the Lowroad,” he says. “Then you have already seen one of them.”
   He lowers his voice. “Farmer Eldric has reported dying fields, frightened cattle,
   and a scarecrow that will not remain where he leaves it. Last night, he found it
   facing his bedroom window.”


Harlowe offers 25 gp to investigate Eldric’s farm, plus meals and lodging at the
Mudhaven Inn. He does not use the word “curse” in public, but he fears that panic
will destroy Greymoor as surely as crop failure.

Roleplaying Mayor Harlowe
• Voice: tired authority, measured words, urgency concealed beneath civic
  restraint.
• Ideal: Greymoor survives because neighbors act before fear takes hold.
• Bond: he promised the families of Greymoor he would keep their children safe.
• Secret: he has received two reports of sheep speaking, but has withheld them to
   prevent panic.

Part 3: The Silent Scarecrow
Farmer Eldric and his daughter Mira meet the characters at their farm. Eldric is
stubborn, exhausted, and ashamed that he is afraid of his own field. Mira is
observant and insists that the scarecrow moves only when no one looks directly at
it.
                                    ELDRIC’S FIELD
   Rows of corn sag inward around a thirty-foot circle of gray, brittle soil. At its
   center stands a scarecrow of burlap, straw, and dark wood. It faces away from
   you. No wind moves its coat. The field smells of split stems and something
   chemical beneath the soil. Then, from somewhere inside the corn, a dry stalk
   snaps.


Investigation
• DC 11 Nature: the plants have been drained of vitality rather than diseased.
• DC 12 Arcana: residual transmutation magic radiates from the soil in a
  geometric pattern.
• DC 13 Investigation: the scarecrow post bears the same lattice scar found on the
  wolf.
• A character who watches the scarecrow while another looks away notices that
  its head has changed angle when attention returns.


• Digging beneath the post reveals a thumb-sized crystal filament connected to
  dead roots. Removing it ends the field’s pulse.
Development: At night, the scarecrow rotates to face whichever character carries the
wolf’s scarred hide or the crystal filament. It is not alive; the lattice uses it as a marker
and observation point.


                                       THE FIRST LINK
  Eldric turns the crystal fragment over in his weathered hands, then drops it as
  a faint green pulse runs through its center. “That thing was in my soil,” he
  whispers. Beyond him, the scarecrow’s empty burlap face now points toward
  the road leading deeper into Greymoor.


Part 4: The Mutated Pool
Clues from the farm, apothecary, and Fletcher’s Cottage lead a quarter mile
upstream to a shaded bend where the water glows under moonlight.
                                  THE STREAM BEND
   The stream widens into a shallow pool beneath leaning willows. Green light
   drifts below the surface in slow pulses. Fish crowd the edges, their gills
   opening in doubled pairs. A flat stone at the center of the pool rises like a lid—
   and the reeds around you begin to shake.


Encounter. Three giant rats and one swarm of insects attack. Their extra eyes and
hardened growths are cosmetic. The swarm emerges at the end of the first round
unless the characters disturb the reeds first.

Discovery
Beneath the flat stone lies a crystalline shard etched with geometric runes. It is
inert but magical. When a creature first touches it, that creature hears a calm
whisper inside its thoughts: “Refinement requires iteration.” A DC 15 Arcana check
confirms directed transformation rather than natural mutation.

Part 5: The Well That Whispers
                                  THE WELL AT DUSK
   The market square empties as dusk settles. The central well stands beneath a
   crooked wooden frame, its rope swaying although the air is still. From below
   comes a wet scrape, then a breath drawn through stone. A bucket rises coated
   in gray slime. Something whispers from the dark—not in words, but in the
   cadence of a voice trying to learn them.


Encounter. One gray ooze clings to the well lining 15 feet below the rim. A swarm
of rats bursts from a side crack when the ooze is first damaged. The narrow well
makes ranged attacks difficult; a creature climbing without a secured rope must
succeed on a DC 10 Athletics check.

Developments
 • The ooze has consumed a lattice-tainted runoff deposit and is not a designed
    Krasis.
 • The rats flee if reduced below half hit points after the ooze is destroyed.
 • Cleaning the lowest stones and sealing the runoff crack stops the whispering.
 • A silver ring worth 15 gp lies in the ooze’s residue; it belonged to a missing
   farmhand.

Part 6: Harvest Moon Howl
                                  THE MOONLIT PACK


   The harvest moon hangs enormous above Eldric’s fields. Three wolves move
   along the crop line, low and silent. A fourth shape rises behind them, larger
   than a dire wolf, with thornlike growths splitting the skin along its spine. It
   lifts its head. The howl that follows rattles dry kernels from the stalks.


Encounter. Three wolves and one Thornfang Krasis. Use dire wolf statistics for the
Thornfang, with AC 14. Its bite deals an extra 1d4 piercing damage from embedded
thorns. At half hit points, its back splits and the thorns extend, but this is cosmetic.

Tactics and Terrain
 • The wolves use Pack Tactics and attempt to pull characters into the corn.
 • The Thornfang attacks prone targets and guards the escape route toward
    Shepherd’s Croft.
 • The corn is lightly obscured beyond 10 feet and counts as difficult terrain.
 • Tracks after the fight show the pack was circling livestock rather than hunting
    randomly—as if herding prey toward the croft.


Part 7: Milk Gone Black
                                  THE WARPED CALF
   The barn doors stand open. Inside, cattle crowd against the far wall, their eyes
   rolling white. A calf occupies the nearest stall alone. Its hide ripples as though
   something moves beneath it. Thick black fluid drips from its mouth and hisses
   when it touches the straw.


Encounter. One Warped Calf. Use boar statistics and add Acid Spit: ranged weapon
attack, range 20/60 feet, +3 to hit, one target, 1d6 acid damage. The calf fights only
while cornered or when the black grain in its trough is disturbed.

Clues
 • The calf ate grain from a sack marked with a tiny geometric brand.
 • The black fluid is mildly acidic but loses potency after one hour.
 • A DC 12 Medicine check reveals crystal fibers growing through the calf’s
   digestive tract.
 • The sack came from a market shipment handled by the Traveling Peddler.

Part 8: Missing Shepherd
Mayor Harlowe finally admits that Tomas has vanished and that several sheep
now possess too many legs. Tracks from the croft lead to an abandoned upper
pasture and a barn built against a low stone ridge.
                                 THE SILENT PASTURE
   The pasture is silent. Six sheep stand in a loose ring around the barn, their
   bodies misshapen beneath dirty wool. Some have six legs. One turns its head
   farther than its neck should allow. Above them, clinging upside down to the
   barn wall, hangs a thin humanoid figure wrapped in a shepherd’s torn cloak. It
   smiles with Tomas’s face.


Encounter. One Shepherd Krasis and six Krasis Warped Sheep. The Shepherd uses
the cultist stat block and has Spider Climb. The sheep use wolf statistics with 9 hit
points each. They fight without bleating and attempt to knock characters prone for
the Shepherd.

Shepherd Krasis Tactics
 • Begins on the wall or ceiling and throws stones or uses a light crossbow until
   approached.
 • Uses Spider Climb to change surfaces without provoking opportunity attacks
   when possible.
 • Commands the warped sheep to surround isolated characters.
 • At half hit points, whispers: “Evolution requires suffering.”


Developments
• If captured, the Shepherd Krasis cannot explain Rabu Shin’s plan; its mind
   contains repeated instructions and sensory impressions.
• Tomas is no longer recoverable. His memories surface only as fragments in the
   Krasis’s speech.
• A carved lattice sigil in the barn beam matches the wolf, scarecrow, and stream
  shard.
• A concealed packet contains notes on livestock response rates and a route
  toward Stonewake Port.
                               CHAPTER REVELATION
  The last warped sheep falls, and silence returns to the pasture. In the barn, a
  page of precise observations lists dates, temperatures, feeding schedules, and
  mutation rates. At the bottom, one sentence has been written in a different
  hand: “Rural viability confirmed. Begin aquatic strain.”


Optional Greymoor Quests
These quests may be completed before or after Missing Shepherd. Use them to
deepen the mystery, provide additional treasure, or advance characters who
bypass earlier encounters.

Part 9: The Crimson Cornfield
                               THE BLEEDING HARVEST
   A farmer brings down his sickle. The corn stalk opens with a wet snap, and
   dark red fluid runs across his hand. All around you, the field shivers although
   there is no wind. Beneath the rustle comes another sound: roots dragging
   through soil.


Encounter. Two vine blights hide among the corn. They use Entangling Plants to
restrain characters while attacking from opposite rows.
• The field is difficult terrain and lightly obscured beyond 10 feet.
• Destroying two buried crystal seeds ends the bleeding within 1d4 hours.
• Each seed is worth 10 gp to an arcane researcher but risks spreading
  contamination if mishandled.

Part 10: Bandits on the Grain Road
                                 ROADSIDE ROBBERY
   An overturned grain wagon blocks the road. Sacks have been cut open, but the
   spilled grain lies untouched. Four armed figures rise from behind the stone
   wall. Their leader steps forward, one arm swollen beneath leather wrappings
   and ending in a grafted claw. “Leave the wagon,” he says, “and keep your
   hands where I can count them.”


Encounter. Four bandits and one bandit captain with a grafted arm. The captain
gains +1 damage on melee attacks and has advantage on the first attack roll it
makes during the encounter.
• The bandits were paid to steal specific branded grain sacks, not valuables.
• The captain received the graft willingly as payment.
• A letter directs delivery to a traveling peddler outside Stonewake Port.

Part 11: Swarm in the Barn
                                    THE RAT KING
   The barn floor seems to ripple beneath the straw. Then the straw erupts. Rats
   pour between the boards in two black waves, and at their center crawls a knot
   of tails, teeth, and fused bodies moving as a single creature.


Encounter. Two swarms of rats and one Rat King. Use swarm of rats statistics for
the Rat King, increase its hit points to 30, and give it Pack Tactics.
 • A creature knocked prone in the barn is immediately surrounded if a swarm can
   reach it.
 • The Rat King nests above a crystal filament beneath the floor.
 • Removing the filament ends the rats’ unnatural coordination.

Part 12: The Traveling Peddler
Lucien Vale arrives at the market selling miracle tonics. This is primarily a social
encounter. Use the spy stat block if combat occurs.
                                   MIRACLES FOR SALE
   A lacquered wagon rolls into the market beneath chiming brass bells. Its
   owner bows with theatrical grace and lifts a vial of pearlescent liquid. “Aches
   dissolved, vigor restored, and age itself persuaded to loosen its grip,” he
   announces. A farmer drinks. His posture straightens—then the bones in his
   hand crack loudly beneath the skin.


Social Encounter Goals
 • Identify the tonic as a diluted mutation catalyst with DC 13 Arcana or Medicine.
 • Prevent villagers from consuming additional doses.
 • Follow or persuade Lucien to reveal his supplier.
 • Recover his ledger linking Greymoor’s trials to coastal shipments.
Lucien’s Escape. If exposed, Lucien uses Cunning Action to flee through the
market. He carries a smoke pellet that heavily obscures a 10-foot radius until the
start of his next turn.

Part 13: The Broken Plow Curse
                                   THE PLOWS MOVE
   Moonlight glints from an iron blade moving through the field. A plow drags
   itself across the soil with no animal before it and no hand upon its handles.
   Another follows, carving a perfect spiral into the earth. Both stop at once. Then
   their blades turn toward you.


Encounter. Two animated plows. Use animated armor statistics with AC 16, 25 hit
points, and a slam that deals bludgeoning damage.
 • The plows attempt to charge in straight lines and knock characters prone.
 • The spiral they carve is a lattice calibration symbol.
 • A buried metal-and-crystal device worth 40 gp powers both objects.
 • Destroying or removing the device ends the animation immediately.


Concluding Chapter 1
Milestone Advancement
Characters advance to 3rd level when they defeat or neutralize the Shepherd
Krasis, secure evidence of directed mutation, and convince Mayor Harlowe that
the threat is arcane rather than a local curse. If the party has completed only the
minimum encounters, award the level after the final revelation in the barn.

Rewards
 • Mayor Harlowe’s payment: 75 gp for resolving the full livestock threat.
 • Eldric’s reward: preserved food, free lodging, and a family heirloom worth 25
   gp.
 • Old Horn Apothecary: two potions of healing at half price, or one free potion
   after the well is cleansed.
 • Lattice shards: important campaign keys. They are magical evidence, not
   intended for sale.
 • Optional quest rewards: 15–40 gp each, favors from farmers, and improved
   reputation in Greymoor.

Greymoor’s Aftermath
The settlement returns to work, but not to innocence. Crops begin recovering after
the lattice filaments are removed. Ordinary animals remain skittish near the
stream. Mayor Harlowe quietly records every strange report and asks the party to
carry warning to Stonewake Port, where Greymoor’s grain and livestock are
shipped.
                         TRANSITION TO STONEWAKE PORT
   Morning settles over Greymoor Hollow with the sound of ordinary sheep and
   axes striking honest wood. Mayor Harlowe meets you at the village bridge.
   “Stonewake receives everything we grow,” he says, handing over a sealed
   letter. “If whatever did this traveled with our shipments, the harbor needs
   warning.” Downstream, the water catches the sun—and for a single heartbeat,
   flashes green.


If the Characters Leave Early
 • Day 3: two warped sheep attack a farmer.
 • Day 5: villagers barricade homes and trade stops.
 • Day 7: the Shepherd Krasis drives a full livestock outbreak through the market.
 • Stonewake receives contaminated grain, strengthening the aquatic lattice node
    in Chapter 2.

Shepherd Krasis: Encounter Profile
The Shepherd Krasis is not intended as a durable solo boss. Its danger comes from
vertical positioning, the warped flock, and the emotional horror of recognizing
Tomas within the creature. Keep it moving across walls and rafters. Let it repeat
simple phrases as though recalling instructions rather than speaking with full
agency.
 Signature Line: “Evolution requires suffering.” Deliver it quietly, as a statement of
 learned doctrine rather than a battle cry.
