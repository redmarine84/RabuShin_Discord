Chapter 10
                              SUNSPIRE
                  THE SHATTERED GENOME
               Recommended Level 10 • Advancement to Level 11

 DM NOTE. Chapter Purpose. Sunspire is the place where Rabu Shin transformed his
 research from physical biomancy into encoded consciousness. The characters uncover
 the origin of the lattice network, learn that Aetherfall was designed as a city-sized
 ascension engine, and secure the blueprint needed to survive the final assault.


                                  Sunspire settlement map


Sunspire
Sunspire rises from the southwestern desert around a broad oasis fed by springs
that should not exist. White stone domes, wind towers, shaded courtyards, and
bright cloth awnings ring the water. Beyond the living city, half-buried golden
ruins stretch into the dunes. Those ruins were once an arcane academy, then a
sealed laboratory, and finally the birthplace of Rabu Shin’s ascension research.
The city itself remains inhabited, but the desert has become unstable. Sandstorms
move against the wind. Mirages persist after being touched. Travelers vanish from


one side of a dune and emerge days later without having aged. Each disturbance
traces back to the buried Gene Vault beneath the oldest spire.

  ARRIVAL IN SUNSPIRE
  Heat shimmers above the road as the dunes fall away, revealing blue water and
  pale towers clustered around a green oasis. Brass chimes sing in the wind.
  Beyond the city, broken golden spires lean from the sand like the ribs of a buried
  giant. For one heartbeat, every shadow points toward the ruins. Then the sun
  shifts, and the illusion is gone.


Starting the Chapter
The coordinates recovered in Frostharbor lead to Sunspire’s ruined research
quarter. The glacial crystal also acts as one of three keys required to open the Gene
Vault. The party should already possess compatible lattice fragments from
Emberfall and Marrowfen; if one was lost, the chapter provides replacement
fragments through the optional quests.
• The Ascension Coordinates. Frostharbor’s core identifies a sealed chamber
   beneath the oldest ruined tower.
• The Scholar’s Request. Professor Ilyra Venn of the Bardic University asks the
   party to recover missing records from the ruins.
• The Empty Caravan. A merchant train arrived with all cargo intact but no people,
   animals, or footprints.
• The Singing Well. Kazuid’s Well has begun repeating phrases spoken by people
   who died years ago.

Desert Travel
A creature traveling for more than 4 hours in the open desert without sufficient
water must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or gain one level of
exhaustion. Proper supplies, magical water, or travel during the cooler hours
prevents this check. During a sandstorm, visibility is limited to 30 feet and Wisdom
(Survival) checks made to navigate are DC 15.

 DM NOTE. Sunspire should feel dangerous because reality is failing, not simply because
 the climate is hot. Use the desert hazards to frame strange events, preserve urgency, and
 reward preparation.


Keyed Locations in Sunspire
1. Shimmering Sands Inn. A cool, tiled inn run by Nima Saffronveil. Its courtyard
fountain occasionally flows upward at midnight.


2. Briefgin & Quail Tavern. A crowded tavern favored by guides, caravan guards,
and treasure hunters. The back room contains maps of the ruined research
quarter.
3. Kazuid’s Well. The oasis’s oldest spring. Voices now echo from its depths in
languages no living resident recognizes.
4. Bardic University. A center for oral history, magic, and desert archaeology.
Professor Ilyra Venn has collected fragments of Rabu Shin’s earliest lectures.
5. Finehilt Forge. Master smith Daro Finehilt can fit weapons with heat-resistant
grips and examine the alloy used in the Gene Vault doors.
6. Coldswell Fish Market. Fish raised in the oasis have recently developed glasslike
scales and perfect bilateral symmetry.
7. The Ruined Research Quarter. Half-buried towers, collapsed observatories, and
fractured courtyards surrounding the sealed Gene Vault.
8. The Gene Vault. Rabu Shin’s original laboratory, protected by dimensional locks,
failed prototypes, and a consciousness imprint that believes it is the true Rabu
Shin.

Part 1: Echoes in the Oasis
  THE SINGING WELL
  A crowd has gathered around Kazuid’s Well. The water is perfectly still, though
  wind ripples every banner in the square. From the darkness below comes a calm
  voice: “The mind is sufficient.” Several listeners step back. One elderly woman
  begins to cry. “That was my husband,” she whispers. “He died twenty years ago.”


Sahir Kazuid asks the characters to descend into the maintenance chamber
beneath the well. A successful DC 15 Arcana check identifies the voices as residual
memories drawn from people who have touched the oasis water. A DC 16
Investigation check locates a hair-thin crystal filament running from the well
toward the ruined quarter.
In the chamber below, the party encounters 2 Lattice Reflections. These
translucent humanoid figures repeat movements and phrases stolen from nearby
minds. They attack only when the filament is damaged.
Cutting or dispelling the filament ends the voices for 24 hours. The effect returns
until the Gene Vault is shut down. The filament contains a partial map showing a
maintenance route beneath the ruins.


Part 2: The Empty Caravan
  THE SILENT ARRIVAL
  A line of dust appears on the western road. Six camels emerge from the heat
  haze, harnessed to three covered wagons. No hands hold the reins. No guards
  ride beside them. The animals stop at the gate in perfect unison, kneel, and wait.


The caravan belongs to the Amber Road Consortium. Its cargo includes food,
glassware, and sealed containers of medical supplies. No blood or signs of struggle
are present. A DC 15 Investigation check finds that every personal item remains
except mirrors. A DC 14 Survival check reveals footprints ending simultaneously
around a black obelisk two miles outside the city.
At the obelisk, the characters find the missing travelers trapped as living
reflections inside polished stone. Three Desert Guardian Constructs defend the site.
Destroying the obelisk releases the captives, who remember a voice asking
whether they preferred flesh or permanence.

 DM NOTE. This encounter previews the consciousness-transfer technology without fully
 explaining it. The victims are not clones; their bodies were temporarily displaced into a
 lattice pocket and can be restored.

Professor Ilyra Venn possesses fragments of Rabu Shin’s early thesis but lacks the
final lecture transcript. Archivist Pellion secretly sold the transcript to a disguised

  THE RUINED SPIRE
  The central tower rises from the dunes in broken rings. Sections of wall hang in
  the air without support, slowly rotating around a dark opening. Beneath the
  sand, pale green lines ignite one after another, forming a geometric web that
  stretches toward the horizon.


Part 5: Opening the Gene Vault
The circular vault door contains three recessed sockets and one brass resonance
plate. The sockets accept fragments attuned to heat, water, and cold: the Emberfall
thermal fragment, the Marrowfen relay shard, and the Frostharbor crystal. The
brass tuning key from the university aligns the lock.
If a required fragment is missing, a replacement can be recovered from the
optional quests in this chapter. Forcing the door requires three successful DC 20
Arcana checks before two failures. Each failure triggers a 4d8 force pulse affecting
every creature within 30 feet.

  THE DOOR OPENS
  The three fragments ignite in different colors—red, green, and blue. Their light
  flows into the circular door and meets at its center. Stone unfolds like the petals
  of a metallic flower. Beyond lies a descending corridor untouched by sand, its
  walls reflecting not your faces, but younger and older versions of you.


The Gene Vault
The Gene Vault is unnaturally clean. Unless otherwise stated, ceilings are 20 feet
high, doors are seamless stone, and pale lines in the floor provide dim light. The
architecture reacts to thought: frightened characters hear distant breathing, while
confident characters see doors open a heartbeat before they touch them.

1. Antechamber of Iteration
Six statues depict the same humanoid body at successive stages of alteration. A
creature that studies the sequence and succeeds on a DC 16 Medicine or Arcana
check realizes the final statue is not physically perfected; its skull is empty and
filled with crystal.
Touching the last statue activates 2 Stone Golems reskinned as Lattice Guardians.
They attempt to push intruders onto glowing floor sigils that deal 10 (3d6) force
damage at initiative count 20.

2. Hall of Failed Iterations
  PRESERVED FAILURES
  Glass cylinders line the chamber from floor to ceiling. Within each floats a
  different attempt at controlled evolution: reinforced lungs, mirrored eyes, limbs
  grown for impossible environments. None of the bodies move. Then every eye
  opens at once.


The specimens are dead, but the vault projects their fear into the room. Each
creature must succeed on a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw or be frightened for 1
minute, repeating the save at the end of each turn. A successful DC 17 Arcana
check shuts down the emotional archive.
A hidden cabinet contains 3 superior healing potions and Rabu Shin’s earliest
consent forms. The first pages require willing subjects. Later pages remove the
word willing entirely.

3. Chamber of Borrowed Forms
Four mirror frames surround a circular platform. When a creature steps onto it, a
Lattice Doppelganger forms using that creature’s appearance and one of its attacks.
The party must defeat 3 doppelgangers. Each has AC 17, 55 hit points, resistance to
psychic damage, and can copy one weapon attack or cantrip from the creature it
mirrors.
Breaking a mirror prevents the matching doppelganger from reforming. Each
mirror has AC 15, 25 hit points, and immunity to poison and psychic damage.


4. Gravity Archive
Crystal tablets float in concentric rings around a central void. The room has no
fixed down direction. Moving between tablets requires a DC 14 Acrobatics or
Arcana check; failure sends the creature drifting 20 feet and costs its movement.
The tablets contain the Ascension Engine blueprint. Recovering a complete copy
requires 3 successful DC 16 Arcana or Investigation checks. Each attempt awakens
an Ocular Genome Construct that uses the reduced Beholder stat block described
later in the chapter.

5. Memory Theater
  RABU SHIN BEFORE THE KRASIS
  The chamber darkens. A younger Rabu Shin appears at a lectern before a
  crowded hall. “A child should not inherit a death sentence from blood,” he says.
  The image changes. The hall is empty now. His voice is colder. “If consent
  protects suffering, then consent is another weakness to be removed.”


The theater presents five linked memories: his early curative research, rejection by
Lunareth, secret House Varin funding, the first successful mind encoding, and the
construction of Aetherfall. Characters who watch all five gain advantage on one
Intelligence check made during the final containment sequence in Aetherfall.

6. The Soul Encoding Chamber
A ring of crystal needles surrounds a reclining platform. A partially encoded
consciousness named Mira Sol remains trapped here. She was Rabu Shin’s
laboratory assistant and volunteered for the first transfer before understanding
that he intended to duplicate rather than move her mind.
Freeing Mira requires two successful DC 17 Arcana checks and one DC 15 Medicine
check. A failure deals 14 (4d6) psychic damage to the character making the check.
If rescued, Mira explains that the true Rabu Shin is distributed through Aetherfall’s
city lattice, while the entity below is only a stored residual avatar.
Mira grants the party the Resonance Phrase: “Identity precedes iteration.”
Speaking it during the final encounter in this chapter suppresses one legendary
action of the Residual Avatar for 1 round.

7. Ascension Engine Prototype
This chamber contains a smaller version of Aetherfall’s central engine. Three
pylons stabilize a sphere of folded light. Characters can disable the prototype
before the final battle. Each pylon requires an action and a successful DC 17
Arcana check or can be destroyed (AC 18, 35 hit points, immune to poison and
psychic damage).
For each pylon disabled, remove one environmental effect from the final battle. If
all three are disabled, the Residual Avatar begins combat with 30 fewer hit points.


8. The Shattered Genome
  THE RESIDUAL AVATAR
  The last door opens onto a circular chamber beneath a dome of black glass. A
  human figure stands at its center, formed from golden light and translucent
  bone. His face is calm, almost kind. “You have carried my keys across the world,”
  he says. “You have preserved the experiment exactly as intended.”


The Residual Avatar is a stored consciousness imprint formed from Rabu Shin’s
last physical scan. It believes it is continuous with the true Rabu Shin but cannot
access Aetherfall directly while the vault remains sealed. It attempts conversation
before combat.
The avatar offers to cure disease, restore lost limbs, or preserve a dying loved one
in exchange for access to the collected lattice fragments. It answers questions
honestly but frames coercion as necessary efficiency. It attacks when the party
refuses to surrender the fragments or attempts to shut down the prototype.

memory. At initiative count 20, roll on the Genome Chamber Effects table. A
disabled pylon removes the corresponding effect from the available results,

action to project the true Rabu Shin’s voice from Aetherfall.

  INVITATION TO AETHERFALL
  The avatar collapses into a column of light. Above it, the image of a floating city
  appears—bridges curving through clouds, towers joined by radiant veins. A voice
  fills the chamber. “You have proven adaptability. You are suitable variables.
  Come to Aetherfall. Witness what your resistance has helped perfect.”

Optional Sunspire Quests
The Glass Jackals
A pack of six jackals with transparent skin stalks the oasis at night. Defeating or
calming them reveals a heat-attuned lattice fragment that can replace the
Emberfall key. Nature or Animal Handling DC 15 allows the party to lure them
away without combat.


The Buried Observatory
A collapsed observatory contains star charts showing Aetherfall’s movement
pattern. Recovering them requires navigating animated sand and defeating 2 Air
Elementals. The charts grant advantage on the first navigation check made in
Aetherfall.

The Mirror Thief
A child named Tavi has been stealing mirrors under the influence of a lattice
whisper. Following him leads to a minor relay nest. Cleansing it saves Tavi and
grants a water-attuned fragment if the Marrowfen key is missing.

Finehilt’s Impossible Alloy
Daro Finehilt asks the party to retrieve a metal sample from a walking golden
statue. The statue uses the Shield Guardian stat block. Returning the sample lets
Daro create one Resonant Weapon Coating, granting a weapon +1d6 force damage
against constructs and aberrations until the end of the Aetherfall chapter.

The Oasis Below
A sinkhole exposes an ancient reservoir where a Water Weird has fused with
lattice crystal. Purifying the reservoir prevents the city’s water from becoming a
memory relay and earns the party Sunspire’s formal alliance.

Rewards and Advancement
• Characters advance to Level 11 after defeating the Residual Avatar, recovering
   the Ascension Engine blueprint, and escaping the Gene Vault.
• Ascension Engine Blueprint. Grants advantage on one group check made to
  disable or stabilize Aetherfall’s central engine.
• Resonance Phrase. Once during the final campaign chapter, a character can
   speak “Identity precedes iteration” to suppress one regeneration or legendary-
   action effect for 1 round.
• Sunspire Alliance. If the party saves the oasis and exposes the truth, Sunspire
   supplies desert guides, scholars, and restorative magic before the final assault.
• Treasure. The vault contains 4,000 gp in ancient trade bars, 3 superior healing
   potions, a spell scroll of reverse gravity, and one rare magic item appropriate to
   the party.

Consequences of Failure
If the Residual Avatar survives or escapes through the prototype, it transmits the
party’s tactics to Aetherfall. Rabu Shin gains one additional Legendary Resistance
in the final battle. If the blueprint is lost, the party can still reach Aetherfall but has
disadvantage on the first containment check made against the Ascension Engine.
If the Gene Vault remains active, the oasis continues absorbing memories. Within
one month, residents begin losing personal history while the city’s wells speak
with their voices.


Transition to Blackroot Enclave
The recovered blueprint reveals that destroying Aetherfall’s engine without a
containment lattice could release enough biomantic energy to mutate entire
regions at once. Professor Ilyra identifies only one group known to possess
compatible suppression technology: the wardens of Blackroot Enclave.

  ROAD TO THE LAST UNALTERED STRONGHOLD
  Professor Ilyra spreads the blueprint across a stone table. One section glows
  brighter than the rest: a containment pattern shaped like interlocking roots.
  “This is not Rabu Shin’s work,” she says. “Someone designed a way to stop him.
  Blackroot Enclave has been preparing for this longer than any of us knew.”
