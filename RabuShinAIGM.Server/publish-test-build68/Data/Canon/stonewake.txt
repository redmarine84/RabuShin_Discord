CHAPTER 2
              STONEWAKE PORT
                          TIDES OF BONE
   Recommended Levels 3–4 • Starting Level 3 • Hybrid Milestone Advancement
Chapter Purpose. Stonewake Port expands the threat beyond Greymoor Hollow.
The characters discover that lattice contamination has been deliberately planted
along a major trade route, encounter the first aquatic Krasis strains, and learn that
Rabu Shin is evaluating environmental adaptation rather than spreading random
corruption. Characters should reach 4th level after destroying the harbor lattice
anchor and defeating the Bone-Tide Krasis.


                            Stonewake Port settlement map


Stonewake Port
Stonewake Port is a crowded western harbor of weathered piers, stone seawalls,
fish markets, ropewalks, smithies, shrines, and steep streets climbing toward
Hillwatch Temple. Fishing boats and merchant vessels normally fill the bay. Now
many remain tied at dock. Nets return sliced apart. Fish arrive with doubled gills
and hardened spines. Sailors report green lights moving beneath the water, and
the drifting merchant vessel Grey Tern entered the harbor without a living soul
aboard.
                             ARRIVAL AT STONEWAKE PORT
   Gulls wheel above a forest of masts as the road descends toward Stonewake
   Port. Dock bells ring, sailors shout, and waves strike the seawall in heavy gray
   bursts. Beneath the salt, tar, and fish is another smell—metallic and sharp, like
   blood on a blade. A bell begins tolling from Starboard Pier. Men run toward
   the water. Something has overturned a skiff inside the harbor.


Starting the Chapter
 • Greymoor Connection. A rejected shipment of hollow grain bears the seal of a
   Stonewake merchant, drawing the characters west to investigate contamination
   along the trade route.
 • Mayor Harlowe’s Request. Harlowe asks the party to warn Dockmaster Rennik
   that Greymoor’s stream carried crystalline residue toward the coast.
 • Merchant Escort. A caravan hires the characters after two coastal wagons vanish
   near Stonewake.
 • Hillwatch Summons. Priestess Lyra has received reports of “the tide being
   rewritten” and seeks adventurers who have already encountered lattice magic.
 Chapter Flow: The chapter can be run as a mostly linear mystery: rescue the sinking
 skiff, gather clues at the port, investigate the lighthouse and cursed nets, board the Grey
 Tern, then descend to the harbor anchor. Optional quests can be inserted between major
 parts or used while the party waits for tides and weather.


Keyed Locations in Stonewake Port
1. The Octopus’ Tankard — A loud dockside tavern built around the preserved
skeleton of a giant octopus. Captain Meryn Holt owns the establishment and knows
every ship entering the harbor.
2. Starboard Pier — The busiest pier and the site of recent current shifts, scraped
hulls, and the sinking-skiff emergency.
3. Seafront Fish Market — A maze of stalls where mutated catches are quietly
discarded before customers see them.


4. Hillwatch Temple — A wind-beaten temple and lighthouse complex
overlooking the bay. High Priestess Lyra studies the spiritual and magical
disturbance in the tide.
5. Hiltsrick Smithy — A respected marine forge. Hiltsrick has found fractal
corrosion in anchors and hull fittings.
6. Rigid Brick Smithy — Hiltsrick’s rival. The owner dismisses supernatural
explanations but has secretly stockpiled warped metal samples.
7. Foarncloak Apothecary — An apothecary specializing in seasickness, venom,
and waterborne illness. The owner can identify strange salts and tissue samples.
8. Seaworthy Inn — A quieter lodging house used by merchants. Survivors and
witnesses can be found here.
9. Port Bazaar — A crowded market of imported goods. Smugglers, relic dealers,
and House Varin agents operate among legitimate merchants.
10. The Grey Tern — A merchant vessel drifting at anchor beyond the inner pier.
Its crew is missing and its cargo hold contains the largest clue in the chapter.

Part 1: The Sinking Skiff
                              CRISIS AT STARBOARD PIER
   A fishing skiff rolls violently beside Starboard Pier. One sailor clings to the
   overturned hull while another disappears beneath the water. Dockworkers
   throw ropes, but the current pulls sideways—toward the center of the harbor
   instead of out to sea. Beneath the gray surface, a pale shape circles once,
   trailing a faint green glow.


Immediate Objective. Rescue both sailors before the skiff is dragged under. Run
this as a three-round hazard encounter. Each round, one character can attempt a
rescue action while others control ropes, repel creatures, or enter the water.
 • Athletics DC 12: Haul a sailor or the skiff 10 feet toward the pier.
 • Acrobatics DC 13: Leap onto the unstable hull without falling prone.
 • Survival DC 12: Read the current and direct the dockworkers, granting
    advantage to the next rescue check.
 • A spell that controls water, grants movement, or creates a secure line
   automatically earns one success if used sensibly.
 • Three successes rescue both sailors. One failure causes a character on the skiff to
    make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or fall into the water.
Hostile Creature. A giant crab with glassy green growths attacks anyone entering
the water. Use the giant crab stat block. It retreats after taking 10 damage or when
both sailors are safe.
                                    AFTER THE RESCUE
   The current releases all at once. The skiff slams against the pier, and the
   rescued sailor coughs seawater across the boards. Wrapped around his ankle
   is a strand of translucent weed threaded with tiny green crystals. “That
   current,” he gasps. “It pulled from below.”


Dockmaster Rennik arrives within minutes. He offers 20 gp for immediate help and
promises another 80 gp if the party can identify and end the source of the harbor
disturbance.

Roleplaying Dockmaster Rennik
 • Voice: clipped commands, sailor’s profanity, concern hidden behind irritation.
 • Ideal: the harbor survives only when everyone does their duty.
 • Bond: Stonewake fed his family for three generations.
 • Secret: he ordered the Grey Tern left untouched because the first boarding party
    heard voices below deck and fled.

Part 2: Ghost in the Lighthouse
                                  THE DARKENED BEACON


   The path to Hillwatch Temple climbs above the harbor through salt-stunted
   grass. The lighthouse beam sweeps across the waves, flickers green, and dies.
   A moment later it returns—pointing inland. From the tower comes the slow
   ringing of a handbell, though the door hangs open and no one stands inside.


Keeper Maelis is alive but trapped in the lens chamber by an animated
concentration of seawater. She altered the beacon crystal after noticing its green
pulse, and the bound energy reacted violently.
Encounter. Use one water weird. The creature remains bound to the circular lens
basin. It attempts to restrain a character and drag that character toward the open
maintenance shaft. A creature falling into the shaft drops 20 feet to a lower
platform and takes normal falling damage.
Disabling the Beacon. A character adjacent to the lens can use an action to
attempt a DC 14 Arcana or Religion check. Two successes suppress the green
resonance, reducing the water weird’s AC by 2 and preventing it from restraining
more than one creature at a time.
                                   THE HIDDEN LINE
   As the final green pulse fades, the lighthouse lens clears. Far below, the harbor
   briefly appears transparent. A geometric line of light runs from the Grey Tern
   to a point beneath Starboard Pier, then vanishes into darkness.


Maelis gives the party a brass lighthouse key, two flasks of holy water, and a hand-
drawn chart marking where the green line vanished.

Part 3: Salt-Cursed Nets
                                 THE MARKET CATCH
   The Seafront Fish Market has gone quiet. A wide fishing net lies stretched
   across three tables, stiff with white salt and tangled around dozens of dead
   fish. Each fish has two sets of gills. In the center of the net, something moves
   beneath the pile.


The net animates when touched. Use a rug of smothering stat block, but describe it
as salt-encrusted rope animated by lattice residue. Replace its suffocating damage
description with abrasive salt and tightening knots; its mechanics remain
unchanged.
Clues. A DC 13 Nature check confirms the fish adaptations improve oxygen intake.
A DC 14 Arcana check identifies deliberate transmutation. A successful DC 12
Investigation check finds a bone-white scale marked with the same geometry seen
in Greymoor.


Fishmonger Ansel witnessed a pale humanoid creature cut the nets from beneath
the water. It moved toward the cracked eastern dock and carried a staff made of
fused bone.

Part 4: The Cracked Dock
                                 DAMAGE FROM BELOW
   The eastern dock leans toward the water. Every piling bears a matching
   fracture, as if struck at the same instant from below. Barnacles have grown in
   precise spirals around the cracks. Beneath the boards, something clicks slowly
   against the wood.


Characters inspecting beneath the dock discover three sahuagin scouts searching
for lattice fragments. They are not allies of Rabu Shin; the altered currents have
driven them from deeper water. The encounter can become combat or negotiation.
• A character who speaks Aquan or succeeds on a DC 14 Persuasion check can
  prevent combat.
• The sahuagin call the harbor node “the green heart” and fear the priest-creature
   guarding it.
• They have seen two giant octopus-like Krasis feeding near the anchor.
• If attacked, they fight until one is reduced to half hit points, then attempt to
   escape into the harbor.
 Negotiated Aid: If the party earns the scouts’ trust, the sahuagin reveal a submerged
 access tunnel beneath the Grey Tern. This grants advantage on the first navigation check
 during the Harbor Depth sequence.


Part 5: The Grey Tern
                              THE ABANDONED VESSEL
   The Grey Tern rocks gently beyond the inner harbor, sails furled and anchor
   chain taut. No watch answers your approach. A meal remains set on the stern
   table, silverware stained black by salt. Every cabin door is closed. From below
   deck comes a slow scrape, followed by three knocks from inside the hull.


The vessel is divided into four keyed areas. Characters can approach by Rennik’s
launch, swim from the pier, or use the submerged route described by the sahuagin.
A. Main Deck — No bodies are present. A DC 13 Investigation check finds drag
marks ending at a hatch. The ship’s log records increasingly strong currents and a
green light beneath the hull.
B. Crew Quarters — Bunks are undisturbed. A locked sea chest contains 35 gp, a
healer’s kit, and a letter warning the captain not to unload Silverreach cargo
without a House Varin agent present.
C. Cargo Hold — Crates have burst from within. Two swarms of quippers occupy
waist-deep water. The quippers retreat if the water is drained or purified.
D. Bilge and Lattice Anchor — A crystalline growth has pierced the hull without
admitting seawater. It pulses in time with the harbor current. Removing it
awakens the creatures below.
The Missing Crew. The crew was dragged through the bilge breach into the
submerged tunnel. Three bodies can later be found near the harbor anchor. One
sailor, Mara Venn, remains alive in an air pocket and can be rescued during Part 6.

The Lattice Anchor
                                THE SEEDED HARBOR
   The bilge glows beneath ankle-deep water. A crystal mass the size of a barrel
   has grown through the timbers, yet no water leaks around it. Thin green veins
   spread across the hull in perfect branching patterns. When you approach, a
   whisper forms inside your thoughts: “Aquatic strain viable. Continue
   pressure.”


The anchor can be detached with three successful checks before two failures: DC
15 Arcana, DC 14 Athletics, or DC 14 thieves’ tools. Each check represents one
minute. On a failure, the anchor pulses and each creature in the bilge takes 3 (1d6)
psychic damage.
Once detached, the anchor opens a circular gap into the submerged access tunnel.
The party can rest before descending, but the harbor worsens if they delay more
than eight hours.


Part 6: The Harbor Depth


                          Harbor Depth tactical encounter map


                                   THE GREEN HEART
   Cold water closes overhead as you follow the glowing fracture beneath the
   ship. The tunnel opens into a drowned plaza of broken docks and ancient
   stone. Two swollen shapes drift between the pilings, each resembling an
   octopus reinforced with bone and crystal. On a shelf of black rock stands a tall,
   scaled figure holding a staff of fused ribs. Green light burns behind its eyes.


The Harbor Depth contains two Giant Octopus Krasis and one Bone-Tide Krasis.
Unless the party used magic to breathe underwater, Hillwatch Temple provides
four potions of water breathing before the descent. Creatures without a swimming
speed follow the normal underwater combat rules.

Battlefield Features
• Broken Pilings. A creature adjacent to a piling has half cover from ranged
  attacks.
• Air Pocket. Mara Venn is trapped in a pocket beneath an overturned launch.
  Reaching her requires an action and a DC 12 Athletics check.
• Lattice Heart. The central crystal has AC 15, 35 hit points, immunity to poison
  and psychic damage, and vulnerability to thunder damage. Destroying it ends
  all lair pulses.


 • Strong Current. At initiative count 20, each creature not holding a fixed object
    must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be moved 10 feet toward the
    lattice heart.
 • Clouded Water. When a Giant Octopus Krasis uses Ink Cloud, the area is heavily
    obscured until the end of its next turn.

Enemy Tactics
 • The octopus Krasis begin hidden among pilings and attempt to grapple lightly
    armored characters.
 • The Bone-Tide Krasis remains near the lattice heart, using Tidecall to reposition
    enemies and Bone Spear against spellcasters.
 • If the lattice heart is reduced below half hit points, the Bone-Tide Krasis orders
    one octopus to shield it while the other attacks the nearest character.
 • At 20 hit points or fewer, the Bone-Tide Krasis attempts to merge with the lattice
   heart, regaining 15 hit points unless a character adjacent to the heart uses a
   reaction to interrupt it with a successful DC 14 Arcana, Athletics, or weapon
   attack roll.
                            THE BONE-TIDE KRASIS SPEAKS
   The priest-creature raises its ribbed staff. “Surface forms fail beneath
   pressure,” it says in a voice like stones rolling under surf. “Rabu Shin provides
   correction.” The green crystal behind it beats once, and every current in the
   chamber turns toward you.


Ending the Battle
When the Bone-Tide Krasis and lattice heart are destroyed, the altered current
collapses. Characters have three rounds to leave the chamber before debris seals
the tunnel. A character can use an action to free Mara Venn. If the party rescued
her, Stonewake’s merchants later add 50 gp to the reward.
                                   THE TIDE BREAKS
   The crystal fractures with a sound too deep to hear. Green light races through
   the drowned stones, then goes dark. For a heartbeat the harbor becomes
   utterly still. The current reverses, rushing toward open sea, and above you the
   bells of Stonewake begin to ring.


Optional Quests in Stonewake Port
Part 7: Smuggler’s Hold
A warehouse near the Port Bazaar contains crates of alchemical salts and
preserved Krasis tissue. Four bandits and one spy guard the cargo. Shipping
ledgers connect the operation to a Silverreach intermediary and reference “urban
adaptation materials.” The smugglers surrender if their leader is reduced below
half hit points and offered protection from House Varin.
Reward. 75 gp in confiscated goods, one potion of climbing, and evidence that can
later grant advantage during an investigation in Silverreach.

Part 8: Mutant Seagulls
A flock of six aggressive gulls attacks the fish market. Use two swarms of ravens,
describing the birds as salt-crusted gulls with translucent wing membranes. The
flock has been feeding on discarded mutated fish. Capturing or studying one
reveals that the corruption spreads through consumption but fades when
separated from an active lattice node.
Reward. The fishmongers provide 25 gp and enough preserved rations for ten
days.

Part 9: Shipboard Plague
The merchant ship Dawn Petrel is quarantined after crew members develop
hardened skin and fever. The condition is caused by powdered lattice crystal
hidden inside medicinal tea. Characters can identify the contamination with a DC
14 Medicine or Investigation check and cure six sailors using lesser restoration,
appropriate magic, or a three-success DC 13 Medicine challenge.
Complication. The ship’s surgeon knowingly accepted the powder from a masked
buyer. He flees toward the bazaar if confronted.

Part 10: Pearl Heist
A sacred pearl used by Hillwatch Temple to calm storms has been stolen by a relic
dealer. Recovering it requires tracking the thief through the bazaar, negotiating
with dockside criminals, or infiltrating a guarded counting house. Returning the
pearl allows High Priestess Lyra to transform it into the Pearl of Mutation
Resistance.
Pearl of Mutation Resistance. While carrying this uncommon wondrous item, a
creature has advantage on saving throws against effects that would alter its form.
Once per long rest, when the bearer fails such a saving throw, it can reroll the save
and must use the new result.


Part 11: Storm Below
After the harbor anchor is damaged but before it is destroyed, a localized storm
forms directly above the bay. Run a four-success skill challenge using Athletics,
Arcana, Nature, Survival, or vehicle (water) proficiency. On three failures, one
dock structure is destroyed and the final Harbor Depth encounter begins with the
lattice heart at full temporary power, granting each Krasis 10 temporary hit points.


Chapter Resolution
Rewards
 • Dockmaster Rennik pays 100 gp for ending the disturbance, plus 50 gp if Mara
   Venn survives.
 • Hillwatch Temple grants the Pearl of Mutation Resistance if the Pearl Heist was
   completed; otherwise Lyra gives two potions of water breathing and two
   potions of healing.
 • Captain Meryn Holt offers free passage aboard a merchant ship traveling toward
    Emberfall or Silverreach.
 • Recovered Silverreach correspondence becomes evidence for Chapter 7.

Milestone Advancement
Characters advance to 4th level when they destroy the lattice heart, defeat the
Bone-Tide Krasis, and prevent the harbor from collapsing into panic. If the party
bypasses the final battle through exceptional planning, award the milestone when
the harbor node is permanently neutralized and its purpose is understood.

What the Characters Learn
 • The mutations are being caused by placed lattice devices, not a natural disease.
 • The devices test adaptations suited to specific environments.
 • Rabu Shin or his agents can communicate through the lattice network.
 • Silverreach financiers and trade routes are involved in moving research
    materials.
 • Similar disturbances have been reported around Emberfall’s volcanic mines.
                                TOWARD EMBERFALL
   By morning, the harbor has changed. Fishing boats leave their moorings.
   Market stalls reopen. The metallic taste beneath the salt is gone. Yet among the
   recovered cargo is a heat-blackened shipping seal from Emberfall and a note
   written in precise geometric script: “Aquatic pressure confirmed. Proceed with
   thermal strain.”

Giant Octopus Krasis
Large aberration, unaligned
 Armor Class 14 (crystalline hide) | Hit Points 76 (8d10 + 32) | Speed 10 ft.,
 swim 60 ft.
 STR 18 (+4) DEX 13 (+1) CON 18 (+4) INT 5 (-3) WIS 12 (+1) CHA 4 (-3)
 Saving Throws. Con +6; Skills Perception +3, Stealth +5; Damage Resistances
 acid; Senses darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 13; Languages —; Challenge 3
 (700 XP).
 Amphibious. The Krasis can breathe air and water.
 Adaptive Camouflage. The Krasis has advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks
 while underwater or against stone and timber surfaces.
 Crystalline Grip. A creature grappled by the Krasis takes 3 (1d6) piercing
 damage at the start of each of its turns.
 ACTIONS
 Tentacles. Melee Weapon Attack: +6 to hit, reach 15 ft., one target. Hit: 13 (2d8
 + 4) bludgeoning damage, and the target is grappled (escape DC 14). Until this
 grapple ends, the target is restrained, and the Krasis cannot use Tentacles on
 another target.
 Acid Jet (Recharge 5–6). The Krasis sprays acid in a 30-foot line that is 5 feet
 wide. Each creature in the line must make a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw,
 taking 18 (4d8) acid damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success.
 Ink Cloud (Recharges after a Short or Long Rest). A 20-foot-radius cloud of
 dark green ink extends around the Krasis underwater. The area is heavily
 obscured for 1 minute or until dispersed by a strong current.


Bone-Tide Krasis
Medium aberration, lawful evil
 Armor Class 16 (bone lattice) | Hit Points 110 (13d8 + 52) | Speed 30 ft.,
 swim 40 ft.
 STR 15 (+2) DEX 14 (+2) CON 18 (+4) INT 13 (+1) WIS 16 (+3) CHA 14 (+2)
 Saving Throws. Con +7, Wis +6; Skills Arcana +4, Perception +6, Religion +4;
 Damage Resistances cold, necrotic; Senses darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception
 16; Languages Aquan, Common, telepathy 60 ft.; Challenge 6 (2,300 XP).
 Amphibious. The Krasis can breathe air and water.
 Lattice Conduit. While within 30 feet of an active lattice structure, the Krasis
 regains 5 hit points at the start of its turn. Radiant damage suppresses this trait
 until the start of its next turn.


Tide Walker. The Krasis ignores difficult terrain caused by water, mud, or
unstable docks.
ACTIONS
Multiattack. The Krasis makes two attacks: two Bone Spear attacks, or one
Bone Spear and one Claw attack.
Bone Spear. Melee or Ranged Weapon Attack: +5 to hit, reach 5 ft. or range
30/90 ft., one target. Hit: 9 (2d6 + 2) piercing damage plus 4 (1d8) necrotic
damage.
Claw. Melee Weapon Attack: +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 11 (2d8 + 2)
slashing damage.
Tidecall (Recharge 5–6). Each creature of the Krasis’s choice within 30 feet
must succeed on a DC 14 Strength saving throw or be pulled up to 15 feet
toward a point the Krasis can see and knocked prone. A submerged creature
makes this save with disadvantage.
Brine of Rewriting (1/Day). The Krasis releases corruptive brine in a 15-foot
cone. Each creature in the area must make a DC 15 Constitution saving throw,
taking 22 (4d10) acid damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. On
a failure, the creature also cannot regain hit points until the start of the Krasis’s
next turn.
REACTIONS
Bone Ward. When the Krasis is hit by an attack it can see, it adds 3 to its AC
against that attack, potentially causing the attack to miss. The ward shatters
after use and cannot be used again until the start of the Krasis’s next turn.
