CHAPTER 4
                         LUNARETH
           THE THEORY OF PERFECTION
   Recommended Levels 5-6 • Starting Level 5 • Hybrid Milestone Advancement
Chapter Purpose. Lunareth reveals Rabu Shin’s origin as a brilliant biomancer
whose research was once supported by the Argent Conclave. The characters
investigate sealed archives, a pollen-borne mutation crisis, and a destabilized ley
junction beneath the city. Their search culminates in a battle against a Runebound
Savant and a Krasis Treant grown from a failed imitation of Rabu Shin’s lattice
theory. Characters should reach 6th level after securing the original thesis,
stabilizing Lunareth’s leyline, and forcing the Conclave to acknowledge its role in
the threat.


                              Lunareth settlement map


Lunareth
Lunareth is an elven city hidden within an ancient forest where silver bridges
curve between colossal trees and crystalline spires rise through the canopy. The
city’s magic is disciplined, elegant, and deeply woven into living things. Lanterns
drift on invisible ley currents. Buildings grow from shaped trunks without
harming them. Song, scholarship, and ritual govern daily life. Yet the harmony has
begun to fracture. Gardens display impossible symmetry. Roots move beneath
paved paths. Several scholars have disappeared, and the Argent Conclave has
sealed portions of its own archive.
                                  ARRIVAL AT LUNARETH
   The forest road narrows beneath branches so immense they hide the sky. Pale
   lights drift between the trunks like slow-moving stars. Ahead, silver walkways
   arc from tree to tree, and towers of crystal rise through leaves shining with
   moonlit color despite the afternoon sun. The beauty lasts until the wind
   changes. A cloud of golden pollen rolls across the lower bridge. Every guard
   nearby pulls a cloth over their mouth. One of them points a spear toward you
   and calls, “Do not breathe deeply.”


Starting the Chapter
 • The Emberfall Inscription. The phrase “photosynthetic aggression viable”
    appears on a secondary crystal recovered from the crucible and points toward
    Lunareth’s living arcane systems.
 • A Scholar’s Request. A messenger from the Argent Conclave asks the party to
   deliver its recovered lattice fragments for examination, but insists they enter
   through a guarded side gate.
 • The Pollen Plague. Travelers along the forest road have fallen ill after breathing
    luminous pollen drifting from Lunareth.
 • A Missing Correspondent. Miner Elra carries a letter from her sister, an
   apprentice gardener in Lunareth who stopped writing after reporting that the
   trees had begun speaking in equations.
 Chapter Flow: The party first deals with the pollen emergency and learns the city is
 hiding a magical failure. Investigation among the Conclave, gardens, bridges, and
 archives identifies a secret circle of young arcanists led by Seredhel. The final descent
 beneath the Whispering Archive combines a living-forest dungeon, a corrupted ley
 chamber, and a two-stage confrontation with Seredhel and the Krasis Treant.


Keyed Locations in Lunareth
1. Greenwood Hearth Inn — A warm inn grown around a living oak. Its
proprietor, Meriel Dawnbranch, keeps a list of missing scholars and frightened
travelers.
2. The Argent Conclave — Lunareth’s governing academy. Archmage Vaelis
controls access to research records and conceals the Conclave’s former support of
Rabu Shin.
3. The Whispering Archive — A layered library carved beneath a crystal-rooted
tree. Its restricted vault contains Rabu Shin’s original thesis and the entrance to the
damaged ley junction.
4. Sylvaris Gardens — Experimental gardens tended by Archdruid Sylvaris. Plants
here are becoming perfectly symmetrical, armored, and aggressively responsive to
magic.
5. The Spire of Reflection — A leyline focus tower where mirrors show possible
magical outcomes. Its reflections now move before the people they imitate.
6. Silver Bridge Network — Graceful bridges carried by magic between trees and
towers. Several suffer brief gravity reversals when the hidden experiment pulses.
7. Moonpetal Market — A quiet market for spell components, living crafts, and
herbal remedies. It becomes the center of the pollen-plague relief effort.
8. The Singing Grove — A sacred grove whose trees normally harmonize with the
moon. The song has changed into a repeating mathematical sequence.
9. Hollow Sentinel — An ancient guardian tree at the eastern boundary.
Something has hollowed its trunk and placed a lattice seed inside.
10. The Ley Chamber — A sealed complex beneath the Archive where Seredhel’s
circle attempted to reproduce directed evolution through arcane lattice
structuring.

People of Lunareth
Archmage Vaelis — A composed elven archmage whose clipped answers conceal
guilt. She funded Rabu Shin’s earliest work and now fears public panic more than
private shame.
Archdruid Sylvaris — The guardian of Lunareth’s living systems. Patient,
observant, and increasingly furious that the Conclave hid biomantic experiments
from him.
Seredhel Amastacia — A gifted young arcanist who believes suppressing Rabu
Shin’s research condemned countless people to preventable disease and weakness.
Meriel Dawnbranch — The practical proprietor of Greenwood Hearth. She knows
which scholars vanished, who returned changed, and which guards have been
bribed.
Ilyra Fenwhisper — An apprentice gardener infected by lattice pollen. She hears
the plants repeating fragments of Rabu Shin’s thesis.


Warden Caladrel — Commander of the Silver Bridge Watch. Loyal to Lunareth,
but willing to defy the Conclave if shown proof that citizens are in danger.

Part 1: Pollen Plague
                                  THE GOLDEN BREATH
     Moonpetal Market has become a field hospital. Elves lie beneath awnings with
     damp cloths over their faces. Golden dust clings to their hair and eyelashes. A
     young gardener arches against a cot as bark-like ridges rise along her
     forearms. Across the square, flowering vines turn their blossoms toward her
     as though listening.


The plague is an adaptive reaction, not a disease. Lattice-tainted pollen carries a
magical command that alters creatures exposed to it. Archdruid Sylvaris asks the
party to stabilize the victims while he traces the pollen to Sylvaris Gardens.

Stabilizing the Victims
Run a skill challenge requiring five successes before three failures. Each character
can contribute once with a particular skill unless circumstances change.
 • Arcana DC 14 identifies the pollen as a transmutation carrier linked to a
   repeating ley pulse.
 • Medicine DC 13 slows the victims’ breathing and prevents further pollen
   absorption.
 • Nature DC 14 produces a neutralizing wash from moonpetal sap and bitterroot.


• Religion DC 15 uses a cleansing rite to separate the victims’ natural life force
  from the imposed pattern.
• Survival DC 13 seals the market and redirects the drifting pollen with wet canvas
   and controlled fires.
• Lesser restoration counts as one automatic success, to a maximum of two
  successes.
 Success and Failure: On success, all victims stabilize and Ilyra can speak clearly. On
 failure, three victims transform into vine-bound thralls and attack using scout statistics
 with a 10-foot thorn lash that deals 1d6 piercing damage. The surviving victims stabilize
 after the combat, but Ilyra suffers disadvantage on checks made to recall the experiment.


                                    ILYRA’S WARNING
   Ilyra’s pupils narrow into leaf-shaped slits. “There are words under the song,”
   she whispers. “Evolution is inefficient because it relies on randomness. He said
   the forest could be taught to choose.” Her fingers tighten around your wrist.
   “Someone answered him.”


Part 2: Sylvaris Gardens
                                    PERFECT GROWTH
   The garden gates stand open. Beyond them, every hedge is the same height,
   every blossom has the same number of petals, and every branch turns at the
   same precise angle. There is no wind, yet the leaves move together. From
   somewhere deeper within comes the padded rhythm of many feet moving as
   one.


Six Vine-Wolf Krasis prowl the gardens. They are wolves fused with vine blights:
silent, coordinated predators able to disappear among foliage. They attempt to
isolate one character at a time and drag victims toward the central root basin.

Garden Encounter Features
• Living Hedges. Five-foot hedge squares provide half cover. A creature can force
  through one by spending 10 feet of movement.
• Grasping Roots. At initiative count 20, one 10-foot square becomes restraining
  roots. Creatures there make a DC 13 Strength save or become restrained until
  the next count 20.
• Pollen Pods. AC 12, 5 hit points. When damaged, a pod bursts in a 10-foot radius;
   creatures make a DC 13 Constitution save or become poisoned until the end of
   their next turn.


 • Moonwell. A creature adjacent to the central pool can use an action and succeed
   on a DC 14 Nature or Arcana check to suppress the garden’s lair effect for one
   round.
Clue in the Root Basin. A crystal seed is wrapped in roots beneath the water. It
bears Seredhel’s personal sigil and a partial activation phrase: “Directed
adaptation, localized trial seven.”


Part 3: The Sealed Truth
The party now has enough evidence to confront the Argent Conclave. Vaelis
initially claims the garden event is an isolated student error. Persuasion,
intimidation, or presenting the Emberfall and garden fragments forces her to
reveal the truth.
                                    VAELIS CONFESSES
   Vaelis closes every door with a gesture. The room darkens until only the
   crystal shard between you gives light. “Rabu Shin was the most precise mind
   Lunareth produced in three centuries,” she says. “We funded him. We praised
   him. Then he demonstrated that a living creature could be rewritten without
   consent, and half the Conclave asked how quickly the method could be scaled.”
   She looks away. “We did not destroy his work. We buried it.”


Rabu Shin’s Original Thesis
 • Natural evolution is slow because it depends on random variation and
   generational death.
 • Arcane lattice structures can identify environmental pressure and impose a
   useful adaptation immediately.
 • A sufficiently complex relay can preserve an adaptation pattern and reproduce
   it across distant populations.
 • The subject’s will is treated as interference rather than an essential component.
 • The final theoretical stage transfers consciousness into the lattice, allowing the
    designer to guide every active node at once.
 The Ethical Divide: The mathematics is internally coherent. Characters who study the
 thesis understand why brilliant scholars were tempted by it. The horror is not that the
 research is nonsense; it is that its promised benefits require surrendering bodily
 autonomy and permitting one mind to define perfection for everyone.

Optional Quests in Lunareth
The Singing Grove
The grove’s sacred harmony has become a compulsion. Characters who enter must
make a DC 13 Wisdom save or repeat their last movement on their next turn. Two
animated trees attack while a third tree sustains the song. Calming the grove with
a DC 15 Performance, Nature, or Religion check avoids combat and provides the
six-part access sequence.

Hollow Sentinel
The ancient boundary guardian has been hollowed from within by a lattice seed.
The party can remove it through a three-success skill challenge using Athletics DC
15, Nature DC 13, Arcana DC 14, or woodcarver’s tools DC 12. Failure awakens the
Sentinel as a weakened treant with 90 hit points. Success earns Sylvaris’s Ironleaf
Charm, granting advantage on one saving throw against being restrained or
transformed.

Moonlit Duel
Warden Caladrel challenges a character to a ceremonial duel after the Conclave
accuses the party of provoking unrest. This is a nonlethal contest to three
successful attack rolls or combat maneuvers. Winning earns the Bridge Watch’s
support; honorable loss earns Caladrel’s respect. Cheating turns the Watch against
the party during the Archive assault.


Whispering Brook
A brook outside the city repeats the voices of missing scholars. Following the
correct voice requires three DC 13 Insight or Survival checks. The trail leads to a
hidden cache containing two potions of greater healing, Seredhel’s field notes, and
a warning that the ley chamber has developed “root cognition.”

Broken Wardstone
A defensive wardstone flickers beside the eastern road. Repairing it requires 50 gp
in components and a DC 15 Arcana check. If repaired, the ward suppresses
reinforcements during the final confrontation. If ignored, two additional Vine-Wolf
Krasis arrive at the beginning of round 4.


Part 4: Beneath the Whispering Archive
                                  THE LOWER ARCHIVE
     The restricted archive ends at a circular door grown from white roots and
     bands of silver metal. When the six-part sequence is spoken, the roots
     withdraw like fingers. Warm air rises from below, carrying the scent of wet
     earth, old parchment, and something sweet enough to make your teeth ache.
     Far beneath, a voice lectures to an unseen class.


Dungeon Features
 • Light. Pale roots glow with dim silver light unless a room states otherwise.
 • Ceilings. Corridors are 10 feet high. Ley chambers rise 30 to 50 feet.
 • Living Walls. Fire damage dealt to a wall causes roots in the room to thrash; all
   creatures adjacent to a wall make a DC 12 Dexterity save or take 3 (1d6)
   bludgeoning damage.
 • Ley Pulse. At initiative count 20 during combat, roll on the Ley Instability table
   unless the local focus crystal has been disabled.
 • Archive Records. A character spending 10 minutes searching a room can make a
   DC 14 Investigation check to find one useful research note or one incriminating
   record.

Area 1: Hall of Living Catalogues
                                      CATALOGUED


   Shelves rise from floor to ceiling, each grown from a single pale tree. Books
   open as you pass. Ink crawls across their pages to form your names, followed
   by measurements of your height, weight, scars, and heartbeat. At the far end,
   two suits of silver armor turn their empty helmets toward you.


Two animated armors and one helmed horror guard the hall. They demand that all
“unregistered specimens” submit to measurement. A character can deactivate one
construct with an action and a DC 15 Arcana check while adjacent to a catalogue
lectern.
Record Found. The hall contains funding approvals signed by Vaelis and six other
Conclave members. The last entry records Rabu Shin’s expulsion and notes that his
complete thesis was never recovered.

Area 2: Chamber of Failed Symmetry
                                 THE EARLY PROTOTYPE
   Glass cylinders line the room. Inside them float branches, animal limbs, and
   organs arranged in flawless mirrored pairs. One cylinder is broken. Wet
   footprints lead from it to a figure standing motionless beside the wall—a body
   of stitched flesh whose crystal-lined joints bend with impossible smoothness.


A flesh golem attacks when any character approaches the far door. Its appearance
is altered, but it uses the standard flesh golem statistics. Lightning does not heal it;
force damage does. A control console can be disabled with three successful DC 14
Arcana or thieves’ tools checks. Each success reduces the golem’s current and
maximum hit points by 10.

Area 3: The Rotating Root Bridge
A narrow bridge crosses a shaft filled with slowly rotating roots. Gravity turns
ninety degrees every round at initiative count 20. Creatures not secured to the
bridge make a DC 13 Dexterity save or fall 20 feet onto a different surface, taking 7
(2d6) bludgeoning damage. A focus crystal halfway across can be destroyed (AC 13,
15 hit points) to stabilize gravity.

Area 4: Apprentice Refuge
Two frightened apprentices, Lethariel and Oswin, have barricaded themselves in a
records room. They helped Seredhel begin the experiment but fled when the
leyline started answering questions. They reveal that Seredhel intends to merge
the root network with Rabu Shin’s copied pattern, creating a self-correcting
organism beneath Lunareth.
 Roleplaying the Apprentices: They are guilty, terrified, and useful. If treated with
 compassion, they provide the counter-sigil that suppresses the final chamber’s lair action
 for the first two rounds. Threats gain the information but cause them to flee and warn no


 one of the unstable escape route.


Part 5: The Blooming Horror
                                     THE LEY CHAMBER
   The last door opens onto a cavern filled with roots thicker than towers. A
   silver leyline hangs in the air like a river of lightning. Beneath it stands
   Seredhel, one hand pressed to a crystal lectern. Behind him, an immense tree
   pulls its roots free of the earth. Wolf skulls, antlers, and elven faces are
   trapped within its bark. Green light opens across its trunk like a single
   enormous eye.


Seredhel’s Appeal
                               THE RUNEBOUND SAVANT
   “Look at what we waste,” Seredhel says, his voice trembling with exhaustion
   rather than fear. “Children born sick. Harvests lost to a single season. Lives
   spent waiting for chance to produce what magic can provide tonight. Rabu
   Shin’s crime was not discovery. It was refusing to let cowards bury it.” The
   roots behind him tighten around the leyline. “Help me guide this, or stand
   aside while I prove that perfection need not ask permission.”


The characters can attempt to talk Seredhel down before combat. Three successful
DC 16 Persuasion, Insight, Arcana, or History checks before two failures cause him
to hesitate. If Ilyra, the rescued apprentices, or Vaelis is present, one appropriate
argument grants advantage. On success, Seredhel does not fight during round 1
and reveals how to disable the lectern. He still refuses to abandon the experiment
unless the Krasis Treant is defeated.

Final Encounter: Seredhel and the Krasis Treant
 • Enemies. Seredhel, the Runebound Savant, and one Krasis Treant. Add two Vine-
   Wolf Krasis for a party of five or more characters.
 • Crystal Lectern. AC 15, 30 hit points, immune to poison and psychic damage. A
    creature adjacent to it can use an action and make a DC 16 Arcana check; three
    successes disable it. Each failed check triggers a Ley Instability effect.
 • Leyline Arc. At initiative count 20, the treant chooses a 30-foot line between two
   roots. Creatures in the line make a DC 14 Dexterity save or take 10 (3d6)
   lightning damage.
 • Root Platforms. Three raised root masses provide half cover. The treant can
   destroy one with a successful Slam, removing the cover.


 • Counter-Sigil. If the apprentices provided it, the treant cannot use Regenerative
    Photosynthesis or the leyline arc during the first two rounds.
 • Seredhel’s Surrender. When reduced below 20 hit points, Seredhel surrenders if
    the lectern is disabled or if the treant has been destroyed. Otherwise he fights
    until incapacitated.

Ending the Experiment
When the lectern is disabled or the treant falls, the ley chamber begins collapsing.
The party must earn four successes before two failures to stabilize the leyline or
evacuate safely. Appropriate checks include Arcana DC 16, Nature DC 15, Athletics
DC 14, Survival DC 13, or the use of a recovered lattice fragment. On failure, each
character takes 14 (4d6) force damage during the escape, and part of the
Whispering Archive is destroyed.
                            RABU SHIN’S CONFIRMATION
   The silver current bends toward the broken lectern. For one heartbeat, every
   root in the cavern becomes transparent, revealing green geometric light
   running through the forest for miles. A calm voice speaks from no single place:
   “Plant-host compatibility confirmed. Photosynthetic aggression viable.” Then
   the pattern snaps, and the trees scream.


Chapter Conclusion
With the ley junction stabilized, the pollen loses its glow and the city’s living
structures slowly return to natural growth. The Argent Conclave can no longer
deny its connection to Rabu Shin. Public reaction depends on how the characters
handled Vaelis and Seredhel.

Consequences
 • If the party exposes the Conclave publicly, Vaelis resigns and Lunareth begins a
    painful inquiry into arcane ethics. The characters gain strong support from
    Sylvaris and ordinary citizens.
 • If the party keeps the scandal private, the Conclave supplies information,
    spellcasting services, and discreet travel assistance, but several guilty members
    retain power.
 • If Seredhel survives, he can become a remorseful expert, a prisoner whose
    testimony divides the city, or a recurring rival convinced the party destroyed
    humanity’s best hope.
 • If the leyline was not stabilized, portions of Lunareth remain affected by
    random gravity shifts and aggressive plant growth during future visits.


Rewards
• Conclave Stipend. 600 gp divided among the party for ending the crisis and
   recovering the thesis.
• Moonleaf Mantle. One character receives a cloak woven by Sylvaris. While
  wearing it, the character has advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made in
  forests and can cast pass without trace once. The cloak regains this property at
  dawn.
• Ironleaf Charm. Awarded for saving Hollow Sentinel; it can be broken as a
   reaction to succeed automatically on one saving throw against being restrained,
   paralyzed, or transformed.
• Rabu Shin’s Thesis. A unique campaign document required to understand later
  relay nodes. Studying it during a long rest grants advantage on the next Arcana
  check involving a Krasis lattice device.
• Milestone. Characters advance to 6th level after the ley chamber is secured, the
  thesis is recovered, and Lunareth formally recognizes the continental threat.

The Road to High Bastion
                             A FORTRESS UNDER STUDY
   Before dawn, Warden Caladrel arrives at Greenwood Hearth carrying a steel
   message tube dented by claw marks. Inside is a military report from High
   Bastion. Supply patrols are vanishing. Watchtowers have been sabotaged in
   mirrored patterns. Creatures wearing bone and chitin armor have begun
   testing the fortress walls, then retreating before the defenders can respond. At
   the bottom, one sentence is underlined twice: “They are learning our
   formations.”


The report includes a safe route through the eastern mountains and a request from
Captain Valric Stonehand for anyone carrying evidence of the lattice network. The
next chapter begins as the party approaches High Bastion beneath the sound of
warning horns.
